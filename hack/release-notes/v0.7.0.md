## Changes

vens now rates each of OWASP's 16 factors on its own: the model picks named answers to closed questions, and vens computes every level, the severity and the score. `config.yaml` gets a version and four new fields; existing scores and severity gates need revalidation. (#310, #432)

### Breaking changes
- **`config.yaml` needs `schema_version: 2`.** A file without it is refused at load, before any LLM call, with a pointer to the [migration notes](https://venslabs.github.io/vens/reference/config-schema/#migrating). Every existing key keeps its name and values.
- **Unknown keys are refused.** A misspelled key used to be ignored, so `compliance_requirement:` (singular) dropped your frameworks without a word. It now fails at load and names the key and its line.
- **`business_criticality` keeps its values with a new meaning: reconfirm yours.** Each value now declares the worst Financial damage and Reputation damage an exploit of the service could cost, and vens sets both factors from it. `critical` no longer means compliance-required: frameworks go in `compliance_requirements`. [What each value declares](https://venslabs.github.io/vens/reference/config-schema/#contextbusiness_criticality).
- **Declaring `data_subjects` rates every finding on its business impact**, the technical impact otherwise. That can move severities both ways, and with no framework in `compliance_requirements` no finding rates critical unless `business_criticality` is `critical`: a gate on `critical` can stop firing once `data_subjects` is declared. `vens init` and the load warning say so.
- **More calls per run.** A batch can require up to seven question calls, plus adjustment calls for flagged CVEs; retries and splitting can add calls. A mock run on the quickstart's Trivy report at batch size 10 made 41 calls versus 8 with v0.6.0, with at least five times the prompt text, excluding response schemas. These are request and prompt-byte measurements, not provider-cost measurements.
- **Go API**, for code that imports vens's packages: `owasp.OwaspRRVector` and `owasp.FromAggregatedScores` are gone, replaced by the 16-level `owasp.Vector` and `Vector.Rate(basis)`; `riskconfig.FormatForLLM`, `riskconfig.RiskSeverity` and `generator.LLMVulnerability` are gone; `attestation.Builder.AddClaim` takes a `[]string` and `ClaimInput.Reused` is gone; VEX output rejects `outputhandler.VulnRating` values missing `ImpactProvenance` or `ImpactBasis`.

### Your scores will change
Same report, same model, different ratings:
- The vector holds 16 levels instead of four numbers repeated. The severity is OWASP's likelihood × impact matrix, no longer a band of the 0–81 score, and is never `info`. Gate on the severity.
- Skill level, Ease of discovery and Awareness are 9 for every finding, so the likelihood is never LOW: a `low` severity needs a LOW impact.
- Confidentiality, integrity and availability losses come from each CVE's usable CVSS v3 vectors, taking the worst value across packages and sources. Financial damage and Reputation damage use `business_criticality` at the CVE's worst technical loss. Declaring a framework no longer adds two points.
- A field left out takes its worst case, with a warning at load: Loss of accountability 7 or 9 without `traceability`, Intrusion detection 9 without `detection`, no cap on Privacy violation without `data_subjects`. `audit_requirement` and the `ids`, `siem` and `edr` controls no longer move any score.
- A CVE with no usable CVSS v3 vector, such as a Debian `TEMP-` id or an advisory with only a CVSS 4.0 vector (#437), takes the worst technical loss, and on the business basis the declared worst case: it can rate critical. Its `vens:impact-provenance` is `missing-cvss-v3-fallback`; keep those in their own list.

### New in `config.yaml`
- `listener`: whether the deployment accepts connections others open.
- `data_subjects`: how many people's personal data the service can read, `none` to `millions`. It caps Privacy violation and sets the impact basis.
- `detection`: what records an attempt on the network, host and application channels. It sets Intrusion detection.
- `traceability`: what the service records of who did what. It sets Loss of accountability with the model's answer.

Declare what is in place, not what a policy requires: see the [config.yaml reference](https://venslabs.github.io/vens/reference/config-schema/).

### In the VEX
- The metadata carries `vens:rating-policy` `2` once. Each vulnerability carries `vens:impact-basis` (`business` or `technical`) and `vens:impact-provenance` (`cvss-v3` or `missing-cvss-v3-fallback`). The same vector can give different scores and severities on the two impact bases: a tool that recomputes the rating must read the basis.
- `vens enrich` copies the three as `owasp_rating_policy`, `owasp_impact_basis` and `owasp_impact_provenance`.
- Dependency-Track 5.2.2 imports the VEX and keeps each finding's OWASP vector and score as vens wrote them. It does not take vens's severity (a finding keeps the one it had), reads none of the three properties, and its own VEX export leaves the OWASP rating out. See [Send the scores to Dependency-Track](https://venslabs.github.io/vens/guides/dependency-track/).

### Known limits
This scoring did not pass the checks #310 set for it. It ships because its vectors hold levels OWASP defines and its ratings recompute from the vector and the declared impact basis, with three trade-offs the maintainer accepted. They were measured with claude-sonnet-4-6 and gpt-5.4-mini on a 74-CVE scan in three deployment contexts and on 37 test scenarios:
- **Low is hard to reach.** The scenario built to rate low rated medium in 10 runs of 10, where v0.6.0's scoring gave low or info. A low now needs a LOW impact and a MEDIUM likelihood, so low Motive, Opportunity and Ease of exploit answers.
- **Less spread inside one deployment.** All 296 ratings in the measured internal context had business impact 1.75, so only likelihood distinguished them. Some business declarations keep every CVE in one impact band; the [limitations](https://venslabs.github.io/vens/concepts/limitations/) name those declarations.
- **Less repeatable on internet-facing services.** 39 of 74 CVEs kept one band across four runs (two models, two runs each), against 53 of 74 for an earlier design of this rework: Non-compliance and Privacy violation answers move the business impact across HIGH's threshold (#433). Two different models agree on the band more often than with v0.6.0's scoring, in all three contexts.

No human risk assessor has reviewed the bands (#435), and vens does not check reachability: an unreachable CVE keeps the rating its answers give. `vens enrich` copies the markers without checking them: a second `vens:rating-policy`, an unknown one or none passes without a word (#434). The measurements and the decision are in #310.

### Migrating
1. Upgrade every runner that reads the file first, CI included: vens v0.6 and earlier load a schema-2 file without an error and score it the old way.
2. Add `schema_version: 2` at the top.
3. Reconfirm `business_criticality` against what each value now declares.
4. Declare `traceability`, `detection` and `data_subjects` for what is in place, knowing that `data_subjects` moves every rating to the business impact, and review any gate on `critical`.
5. Set `listener: absent` for a job or a client that accepts no connection.
6. Run once and read the warnings. `--strict` refuses a run that would use a worst-case fallback.

Details: [Migrating from vens v0.6](https://venslabs.github.io/vens/reference/config-schema/#migrating), [What changed in v0.7.0](https://venslabs.github.io/vens/concepts/cvss-vs-owasp/#what-changed-in-v070), [Limitations](https://venslabs.github.io/vens/concepts/limitations/).

### Also in
- **`vens init`** asks a short series of questions and writes a schema-2 `config.yaml`, explaining each field it asks. (#389, #432)
- **Signed attestations.** `--attest-signing-key` and `--attest-signing-key-id` add a JSF ES256 signature to the `--attest` file. (#317)
- **`--attest`**: a claim now links to the evidence of every call that answered for its CVE, and its `reasoning` holds one line per question answered. (#432)
- **`--strict`** fails a run that would use a worst-case fallback (an undeclared field, a CVE with no description or no usable CVSS v3 vector, a `cannot_tell` answer) and writes no VEX. (#432)
- **`--debug-dir` writes `answers.json`**: each answer as the model wrote it, how vens read it, and the level it set. (#432)
- A reply vens cannot read is asked again once instead of stopping the run, and counted as `reply_unreadable`. (#432)
- Each question gets one entry per CVE with every affected package, and rows are sorted before batching; retries and splits can ask the CVE again. (#363, #432)
- `vens generate --help` and `vens enrich --help` show working examples. (#420)
