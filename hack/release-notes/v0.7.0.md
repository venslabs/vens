## Changes

vens now scores each of OWASP's 16 factors on its own, and `config.yaml` has a version.

### Breaking changes
- **`config.yaml` needs `schema_version: 2`.** A file without it is refused at load, before any LLM call, with a pointer to the [migration notes](https://venslabs.github.io/vens/reference/config-schema/#migrating). Every existing key keeps its name, values and validation.
- **Unknown keys are refused.** A misspelled key used to be ignored: `compliance_requirement:` (singular) dropped your frameworks without a word. It now fails at load and names the key and its line.
- **`business_criticality` keeps its values with a new meaning: reconfirm yours.** Each value now declares the worst an exploit of the service could cost, as OWASP's Financial damage and Reputation damage at a low, a medium and a high technical impact (`critical`: with a high technical impact, bankruptcy (FD 9) and brand damage (RD 9); medium, a significant effect on annual profit (7) and loss of goodwill (5); low, a minor effect on annual profit (3) and loss of major accounts (4)), and vens sets both factors from it. `critical` no longer says compliance-required: frameworks go in `compliance_requirements`. Every value's declaration is in the [reference](https://venslabs.github.io/vens/reference/config-schema/#contextbusiness_criticality).

### New in `config.yaml`
- `listener`: `present`, `absent` or `unknown` (default). Does this deployment accept connections others open? `present` and `absent` are shown to the model with the rest of the deployment; `unknown` is not shown.
- `data_subjects`: `none`, `one_individual`, `hundreds`, `thousands`, `millions`. How many people's personal data the service can read. It caps Privacy violation; never sent to the model.
- `detection`: `network`, `host` and `application`, each `not_logged`, `logged` or `logged_and_reviewed`, and `active_in_application` for `application`. What records an attempt on each channel, across the whole service. Declare all three, or leave the key out. It sets Intrusion detection; never sent to the model.
- `traceability`: `none`, `per_request`, `per_individual`. What the service records of who did what, as it is, not as required. It sets Loss of accountability with the model's answer; never sent to the model.

### How vens rates a CVE
- vens asks the model one question per model-answered factor, up to 7 of the 16 and only when the factor's contract requires it, each with a closed list of named answers, and computes every level, the severity and the score in Go. The published vector carries 16 levels OWASP defines; the severity is OWASP's matrix; the score is likelihood × impact, a vens number, not OWASP's.
- Skill level, Ease of discovery and Awareness are 9 for every finding, so likelihood is never LOW and `info` never occurs: a Low severity needs a LOW impact.
- The impact is the business impact when `config.yaml` declares `data_subjects`, the technical impact otherwise: vens takes the declared context as true, and declared, it holds the business information OWASP rates on. On OWASP's own example the technical impact says High and the business impact Low.
- Loss of confidentiality, integrity and availability are not asked: vens reads them from the worst `C`, `I` and `A` of the CVE's usable CVSS v3 vectors, confidentiality in the column `data_sensitivity` picks, availability in the one `availability_requirement` picks (low and medium secondary services, high and critical primary). `N` gives 2, 1 and 1; `L` gives 2 (6 on high or critical data), 3 and 1 (5 on primary services); `H` gives 6 (7), 7 and 5 (7). A CVE with no usable v3 vector takes 9 for each, counted as `cvss_v3_missing`. `availability_requirement` left out reads primary, each CVE read from a vector counted as `availability_requirement_missing`. Privacy violation is floored at 3, and not asked, only when the CVE has a usable CVSS v3 vector and the worst `C` they give is `N`: v2 and v4 vectors no longer floor it. That changes 1 CVE of the demo scan, 1 of the quickstart's Trivy report and 4 of its Grype report, each now asked Privacy violation.
- Financial damage and Reputation damage are not asked: vens reads what `business_criticality` declares at the band of the highest of Loss of confidentiality, integrity and availability, high with no usable v3 vector. FD by `low`/`medium`/`high`/`critical` at a low, medium and high technical impact: 1/1/1, 1/1/3, 1/3/7, 3/7/9; RD: 1/1/1, 1/1/4, 1/4/5, 4/5/9. The high column is the declared worst case; the steps down are vens's customization of OWASP's options, not calibrated against experts' ratings, in OWASP's direction: the business impact stems from the technical impact. `notes` and descriptions no longer reach them, and one value sets both.
- No question asks whether a CVE is reachable, no level is set from it and no severity capped, and vens publishes no VEX status: a CVE keeps the rating its answers give.
- Compliance frameworks no longer add 2; the model judges the violation.
- A CVE with no description in the scanner's report is not asked Motive, Opportunity or Ease of exploit: they take their worst level. A title alone does not count as a description.
- A flagged CVE's adjustment asks Opportunity, of a CVE with a description, and confidentiality, of a CVE asked Privacy violation, which `not_affected` lowers to its floor; it moves no technical impact.

### Your scores will change
- **Every existing config scores LAC 7 or 9 until `traceability` is declared.** `audit_requirement` states a requirement, not what is in place: it no longer moves any score, and vens warns when it is set and `traceability` is not.
- **Intrusion detection is 9 on every finding until you declare `detection`.** `ids`, `siem` and `edr` say a tool exists, not that this service's attempts are recorded, so they give no credit; vens warns when one is on and `detection` is absent.
- **Privacy violation has no cap, and every finding is rated on its technical impact, until you declare `data_subjects`**: the model's answer stands. Declared, every finding is rated on its business impact.
- Declaring the worst case (`traceability: none`, every channel `not_logged`, `data_subjects: millions`) gives the same levels, without the warning; declaring `data_subjects` also moves every rating to the business impact.
- **On the business impact, Financial damage and Reputation damage follow `business_criticality`**, not the model. With no framework declared, a HIGH business impact needs `critical`, a high technical impact and `data_subjects` of `hundreds` or more; a `high` service tops out at 5.75, MEDIUM; `low` never reaches HIGH. A `C:H` CVE on public data in a `critical` service gets FD 9 and RD 9, and so does a CVE with no usable CVSS v3 vector.
- `waf`, `ddos_protection`, `segmentation`, `zero_trust` and `antivirus` are shown, with their scope, only where the model judges how easy the exploit is here.

### Runs and output
- `--llm-batch-size` counts CVEs, each sent once per question asked about it. On the demo scan at batch 10, in the internet-facing context with detection undeclared, a run makes 95 calls instead of 13 [stale: counted with the reachability and the three impact questions; cost and time: to measure before the release].
- Each VEX vulnerability carries one `vens:impact-provenance` property: `cvss-v3`, or `missing-cvss-v3-fallback` for a CVE with no usable CVSS v3 vector, whose three technical impacts the missing-input rule set to 9 (the CVEs counted as `cvss_v3_missing`). Keep the second kind in a separate "impact unknown" list; among the others, order by severity, then score, then identifier. `vens enrich` copies it as `Custom.owasp_impact_provenance`. Dependency-Track 5.1 reads neither.
- Each VEX vulnerability also carries one `vens:impact-basis` property, `business` or `technical`: the impact its severity and score are read on, the same for the whole run. The vector alone does not say which, so a tool recomputing the severity from it must read the property. `vens enrich` copies it as `Custom.owasp_impact_basis`; Dependency-Track 5.1 does not read it.
- A CVE's rows are read sorted (package name, installed version, package ID, fixed version, BOM-ref, then the whole row), not in the scanner's order: requests, the flags' evidence and the VEX `affects` no longer move when a scanner lists the same packages in another order. On the demo scan, S1's inputs, the scenarios and the quickstart's two reports the order is the scanner's already, so nothing they send changes.
- `severity_contested_unavailable` counts the same way on Trivy and Grype: a CVE whose severity, on every row, is not NVD's own and that the report gives no NVD severity to check against. Trivy never counted it; Grype counted records that were NVD's own. On the quickstart that is 27 of the Trivy report's 45 CVEs (0 before) and 0 of the Grype report's 33 (5 before); 46 of the demo scan's 74.
- Each vulnerability carries the scoring method's version [carrier to settle and test through Dependency-Track's VEX import before the release: `vulnerabilities[].tools`, or the vens version in `metadata.tools`].
- `--strict` fails a run that would use a worst-case fallback. A scan holding a CVE with no usable CVSS v3 vector or no description in the scanner's report cannot pass it; the error lists every such CVE.
- A reply vens cannot read no longer stops the run at once: the CVEs it leaves unanswered are asked again, once, as when a reply leaves one out, and the run stops only if they are still unanswered. Each such reply is counted as `reply_unreadable`.
- `--debug-dir` also writes `answers.json`: per CVE, each answer as the model wrote it, how vens read it, the level and where it came from (`worst_case` for Skill level, `cvss` for the three technical impacts, with the value read, `criticality` for Financial damage and Reputation damage), and the counters; for the run, its defects.
- Every rating's severity is `low`, `medium`, `high` or `critical`. With vens-action's `fail-on-severity` set, a vulnerability with no OWASP rating, or a severity other than critical, high, medium, low or info, fails the gate [vens-action #13, once merged].

### Migrating
1. Add `schema_version: 2` at the top.
2. Reconfirm `business_criticality` against what each value now declares; `critical` no longer means compliance-required.
3. Declare `traceability`, `detection` and `data_subjects` for what is in place. vens does not derive them from `audit_requirement` or `controls`.
4. Set `listener: absent` for a job or client that accepts no connection.
5. Run once and read the warnings.

A schema-2 file still loads on vens v0.6 and earlier, which ignore unknown keys and score it the old way: upgrade every runner that reads it.

Details: [Migrating from vens v0.6](https://venslabs.github.io/vens/reference/config-schema/#migrating), [What changed in v0.7.0](https://venslabs.github.io/vens/concepts/cvss-vs-owasp/#what-changed-in-v070).
